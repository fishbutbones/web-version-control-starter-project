Types of Corydoras

Corydoras are a small and peaceful species of freshwater catfish that are popular in the aquarium trade. They are native to South America and are known for their distinctive barbs and scute armored bodies. Corydoras display a unique shoaling behavior and are known to 'wink.'

Click each item to learn more about the different types of Corydoras:

Bronze Corydoras are one of the most common types of Corydoras in the aquarium hobby. They are one of the larger species of Corydoras, most reaching upwards of 3 inches as full grown adults. They have a distinctive bronze coloration and some can have a greenish iridescence.

Albino Corydoras are actually not a seperate species of Corydoras, but rather a color mutation of an existing Corydora species. Most albino Corydoras are mutations of Bronze Corydoras, but Albino Peppered Corydoras are also common within the trade.

Peppered Corydoras are a smaller species of Corydoras, most only being around 2 inches as adults. They are known for their distinctive peppered coloration, the black and white peppering on their scutes sometimes looking sparkly under different light.

Panda Corydoras are another small species of Corydoras, being around the same size as Peppered Corydoras. They are known for their distinctive black and white coloration, giving them a similar appearance to a panda.

Sterbas Corydoras are a medium-sized species of Corydoras, typically reaching about 3 inches in length. They are known for their elongated body shape and distinctive coloration, which includes a dark lateral line and spotted patterns on their scutes.

Corydora Lifecycle

Corydora eggs sticking to the glass of a fish tank.

“Corydoras panda” by Wolfgang Brüsehaber, CC BY-NC-SA 2.0

A simple illustration of a Corydora life cycle. Drawing by Orion Nguyen.

Sometimes, when the water conditions are right, Corydoras will lay eggs on the glass. These eggs hatch into fry, or baby corydoras. Depending on the species, it can take anywhere from 3 months to a year for them to grow to their adult size. Adult corydoras then find other corydoras to mate with, and the cycle restarts.
